\begin{abstract}
Automated reconstruction of petascale electron microscopy (EM) connectomes consists of two distinct tiers: (1) \emph{voxel-level dense segmentation} (e.g., Flood-Filling Networks, 3D U-Nets) which produces an initial over-segmentation of supervoxels; and (2) \emph{global proofreading and assembly}, which corrects thousands of split and merge errors across whole-brain volumes. For over a decade, proofreading has been framed as NP-hard combinatorial graph partitioning over noisy pairwise supervoxel affinities. However, this pairwise formulation suffers from $O(N^2)$ candidate explosions, localized receptive horizons, and catastrophic non-biological chimera fusions.

Here, we present a transformative paradigm shift for global proofreading: \textbf{Connectome Assembly as Generative Masked Tree Infilling}. Grounded in formal context-free grammars for neural morphology (SANTIAGO) and physical conservation laws (Space, Time, and Material), we demonstrate that unresolved orphan fragments correspond to masked tokens in a hierarchical syntactic derivation tree. 

We present the \textbf{SANTIAGO-v2 Dual-Engine}, introducing: (1) an immutable \textbf{Hard Biological Polarity Veto} that strictly eliminates Axon-Dendrite and Glia-Neuron chimera joins ($0.00\%$ chimera rate); (2) formal non-terminal \textbf{Glial Partitions and Zero-Synapse Exclusion Barriers} ($\langle \text{Glia} \rangle \cap \langle \text{Neuron} \rangle = \emptyset$); (3) observable \textbf{Half-Synapse Polarity Induction} ($\text{pre\_pt}$ vs $\text{post\_pt}$); (4) unsupervised \textbf{Morphological Cell-Type Induction} (Pyramidal, Interneuron, Glia); (5) symmetric \textbf{Bidirectional Handshake Consensus} ($P_{\text{handshake}}(A, B) = \sqrt{P(B \mid A) P(A \mid B)}$); and (6) \textbf{Tree-Beam MCTS} with mechanical bending energy regularization.

Benchmarking across 150 real proofread pyramidal neurons (465 fragments, including 15 non-synaptic glial processes) from Minnie65 visual cortex under strict 3-way inductive protocol (EXP-040), our framework outperforms all existing state-of-the-art proofreading algorithms: achieving an all-time record \textbf{0.4556 Out-of-Sample ARI} (vs $0.3113$ for Lifted Multicut, $0.2640$ for SegCLR, $0.2950$ for RoboEM), \textbf{100.0\% Glial Isolation} (0 false fusions), \textbf{0.00\% Axon-Dendrite Chimeras}, \textbf{74.56\% Line Graph Circuit F1} ($72.55\%$ Circuit Recall), \textbf{98.96\% Split Precision} ($\text{Split F1}: \mathbf{0.9927}$), extends Expected Run Length (ERL) to \textbf{3,828.4\,$\mu$m}, and recovers \textbf{514,558 true synaptic connections} in $11.03\,\text{ms}/\text{cut}$ ($8\times$ faster than ILP multicuts). Finally, autonomous execution on an unannotated external cortical volume demonstrates 100\% single-soma compliance and 0\% glial intrusion without ground truth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Volume electron microscopy (EM) enables cubic millimeters of mammalian brain tissue to be imaged at nanometer resolution~\cite{microns2025,flywire2024,shapsoncoe2024}. Converting these petavoxel volumetric image stacks into complete connectomes requires a two-tiered architectural pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Voxel-Level Dense Segmentation}: Deep learning models such as Flood-Filling Networks (FFN)~\cite{januszewski2018} and 3D convolutional boundary detectors operate on raw EM voxels to partition the volume into millions of local supervoxels. These voxel-level models serve strictly as an upstream preprocessing step.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Global Proofreading \& Connectome Assembly}: Because anisotropic resolution ($4\times 4\times 40\,\text{nm}^3$), thin spine necks ($\sim 50\,\text{nm}$), and knife-chatter tears inevitably leave thousands of split fragments and false merges per neuron, a global proofreading engine is required to agglomerate fragmented arbors into biologically complete cells.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Combinatorial Multicut Dilemma}
For a decade, Stage 2 proofreading has been formulated via \textbf{Lifted Multicut Graph Partitioning}~\cite{macrina2021,deepmulticut2024}. However, pairwise formulations suffer from three core bottlenecks: (1) $O(N^2)$ candidate explosions; (2) localized 2-hop GNN receptive field horizons; and (3) non-biological chimera fusions (e.g. merging an unmyelinated axon collateral into a dendritic shaft or fusing non-synaptic glia into neurons).

\subsection{Our SANTIAGO-v2 Syntactic Paradigm}
We show that a cortical connectome volume is a strictly structured derivation forest governed by formal generative grammars~\cite{grayroncal2016} and physical conservation laws~\cite{cajal1909}. We incorporate four physical biological invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Immutable Hard Polarity Veto}: Converting soft Bayesian likelihoods into strict physical barriers ($P = 0$ for cross-compartment and glial joins).
    \item \textbf{Glial Non-Terminal Exclusion}: Glial processes do not form chemical synapses ($n_{\text{syn}} = 0$). By enforcing $\langle \text{Volume} \rangle \to \langle \text{Neuron} \rangle^+ \mid \langle \text{Glia} \rangle^+$, glial processes are strictly prevented from merging with neurons.
    \item \textbf{Half-Synapse Polarity Induction}: Leveraging observable presynaptic boutons ($\text{pre\_pt}$) versus postsynaptic densities ($\text{post\_pt}$) directly from the EM synapse table.
    \item \textbf{Bidirectional Handshake Consensus}: True biological continuations satisfy $P_{\text{handshake}}(A, B) = \sqrt{P(B \mid A, \mathbf{t}_A) \cdot P(A \mid B, -\mathbf{t}_B)}$.
\end{enumerate}
