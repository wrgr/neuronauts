\begin{abstract}
For over a decade, automated reconstruction and proofreading of petascale electron microscopy (EM) connectomes has been formulated as a combinatorial graph partitioning problem: scoring pairwise affinities between fragments and solving NP-hard integer linear programming (ILP) multicuts. However, this pairwise formulation suffers from three fundamental bottlenecks: $O(N^2)$ candidate explosions, localized 2-hop graph neural network horizons, and catastrophic non-biological chimera fusions. Here, we present a transformative paradigm shift: \textbf{Connectome Assembly as Generative Masked Tree Infilling}. Grounded in formal context-free grammars for neural morphology (SANTIAGO) and Santiago Ram{\'o}n y Cajal's physical conservation laws (Space, Time, and Material), we demonstrate that unresolved orphan fragments and false cuts correspond exactly to masked tokens in a hierarchical syntactic derivation tree. 

We introduce the \textbf{Cajal-Geodesic Dual-Engine}, combining: (1) continuous-discrete \textbf{3D Tree-Grammar Transformers}; (2) \textbf{Cajal's Law of Space} optimal bifurcation angle priors $\cos \theta = \frac{r_0^2 + r_1^2 - r_2^2}{2 r_0 r_1}$; (3) a \textbf{3D Geodesic Fast Marching EM Voxel Tracer}; and (4) \textbf{Observable Directional Ray Infilling}.

To evaluate real-world robustness under 100\% blind conditions, we report full pairwise Merge and Split contingency matrices ($\text{TP}/\text{FP}/\text{FN}/\text{TN}$) on 150 real proofread pyramidal neurons (450 fragments) from Minnie65 visual cortex under strict 3-way inductive protocol (EXP-035). Our unified pipeline achieves an \textbf{88.81\% Top-3 candidate pool accuracy} across 1,573 blind cuts, a \textbf{41.96\% single-shot Top-1 accuracy}, a \textbf{72.11\% Line Graph Circuit F1}, \textbf{98.36\% Split Precision} ($\text{Split F1}: \mathbf{0.9893}$), extends Expected Run Length (ERL) to \textbf{3,613.2\,$\mu$m} ($+1,478.1\,\mu$m error-free growth), and recovers \textbf{486,924 true synaptic connections} in $8.84\,\text{ms}/\text{cut}$ ($10\times$ faster than ILP multicuts).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Volume electron microscopy (EM) now enables the acquisition of cubic millimeters of mammalian brain tissue at nanometer resolution, capturing hundreds of thousands of cells, millions of dendritic spines, and hundreds of millions of synaptic connections~\cite{microns2025,flywire2024,shapsoncoe2024}. Transforming these petavoxel volumetric image stacks ($>10^{15}$ voxels) into biologically complete wiring diagrams represents one of the grand scientific challenges in modern neuroscience~\cite{grayroncal2016,macrina2021}.

Despite remarkable progress in initial voxel segmentation via 3D flood-filling networks and boundary detection~\cite{januszewski2018,lee2019}, automated reconstructions remain heavily fragmented. Anisotropic voxel resolutions (e.g., $4\times 4\times 40\,\text{nm}^3$), thin spine necks ($\sim 50\text{--}80\,\text{nm}$), densely packed unmyelinated axons, staining artifacts, and cutting knife tears inevitably introduce thousands of split errors (false cuts) and merge errors (frankenmerges) per cell~\cite{turner2023,schlegel2023}.

\subsection{The Combinatorial Multicut Dilemma}
For the past decade, connectome proofreading has been approached through the lens of \textbf{Combinatorial Graph Partitioning} and the \textbf{Lifted Multicut} formulation~\cite{macrina2021,deepmulticut2024,roboem2023}. In this paradigm, an initial over-segmentation is represented as a supervoxel graph $G = (V, E)$. Machine learning models predict pairwise merge probabilities $P(u \sim v)$ for candidate fragment pairs $(u, v) \in V \times V$, followed by an NP-hard Integer Linear Program (ILP) or correlation clustering solver.

While mathematically well-defined on clean local benchmarks, pairwise combinatorial partitioning suffers from three critical deficiencies at petascale:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The $O(N^2)$ Pairwise Explosion}: In a $1\,\text{mm}^3$ cortical volume containing $10^7$ fragmented segments, evaluating all potential bridge candidates within a $15\,\mu\text{m}$ radius generates over $10^9$ candidate pairs, creating an intractable computational bottleneck.
    \item \textbf{The Local Receptive Field Horizon Trap}: Standard Graph Neural Networks (such as GAT or GraphSAGE) propagate messages across 2-to-3 hop neighborhoods ($<5\,\mu\text{m}$). Consequently, the GNN lacks global cell-scale awareness: it cannot determine whether an isolated fragment belongs to an apical dendrite, a basal arbor, or an axon collateral located $150\,\mu\text{m}$ away.
    \item \textbf{Non-Biological Catastrophic Chimera Fusions}: Pairwise classifiers operate without biological grammar constraints. A slight threshold perturbation or noisy EM contact often leads to catastrophic merge errors: fusing an axon collateral directly into an apical dendritic trunk, or bridging two distinct pyramidal somata together into a single non-biological chimera neuron.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Paradigm Shift: Generative Masked Tree Infilling}
In this paper, we propose a fundamental departure from combinatorial partitioning. We show that a cortical neuron is not an arbitrary geometric graph, but a strictly structured hierarchical derivation tree governed by formal generative grammars~\cite{grayroncal2016} and physical conservation laws~\cite{cajal1909}. 

Under this formulation, an unresolved false cut at a branch interface corresponds exactly to a \textbf{masked token} in a 3D syntactic tree sequence. Connectome assembly is thereby reformulated as \textbf{Generative Masked Tree Infilling}. By restricting the solution space to grammatically derivable transitions, our method physically eliminates non-biological chimera merges ($0.00\%$ syntax violation rate under perfect typing) while computing candidate continuation probabilities in linear time ($O(N)$).

\section{Theoretical Foundations: SANTIAGO Grammar \& Biological Invariance}

\subsection{The SANTIAGO Grammar Hierarchy}
Extending the foundational work of Gray-Roncal~\cite{grayroncal2016} on grammar-based neurocartography, we formalize the structural topology of vertebrate pyramidal neurons as a 3D Probabilistic Context-Free Grammar $G = (V_N, V_{\Sigma}, R, S, P)$:
\begin{align}
\langle \text{Neuron} \rangle &\to \langle \text{Soma} \rangle \, \langle \text{ApicalTree} \rangle \, \langle \text{BasalTree} \rangle^+ \, \langle \text{AxonArbor} \rangle \label{eq:p1} \\
\langle \text{ApicalTree} \rangle &\to \langle \text{ApicalTrunk} \rangle \, \langle \text{ApicalFork} \rangle \label{eq:p2} \\
\langle \text{ApicalFork} \rangle &\to ( \, \langle \text{ApicalTree} \rangle \, , \, \langle \text{ApicalTree} \rangle \, ) \mid \langle \text{ApicalTuft} \rangle \label{eq:p3} \\
\langle \text{BasalFork} \rangle &\to ( \, \langle \text{BasalTree} \rangle \, , \, \langle \text{BasalTree} \rangle \, ) \mid \langle \text{BasalTerminal} \rangle \label{eq:p4} \\
\langle \text{AxonFork} \rangle &\to ( \, \langle \text{AxonArbor} \rangle \, , \, \langle \text{AxonCollateral} \rangle \, ) \mid \langle \text{AxonTerminal} \rangle \label{eq:p5} \\
\langle \text{Dendrite} \rangle &\to \langle \text{Shaft} \rangle \, \langle \text{Spines} \rangle, \quad \langle \text{Spines} \rangle \to \langle \text{Spine} \rangle^+ \label{eq:p6} \\
\langle \text{Spine} \rangle &\to \langle \text{SpineHead} \rangle \, \langle \text{Synapse} \rangle \label{eq:p7} \\
\langle \text{Axon} \rangle &\to \langle \text{AxonTrunk} \rangle \, \langle \text{Boutons} \rangle, \quad \langle \text{Boutons} \rangle \to \langle \text{Bouton} \rangle^+ \label{eq:p8} \\
\langle \text{Bouton} \rangle &\to \langle \text{Varicosity} \rangle \, \langle \text{Synapse} \rangle \label{eq:p9}
\end{align}

\subsection{Cajal's Laws of Morphological Conservation}
We incorporate three physical conservation principles discovered by Santiago Ram{\'o}n y Cajal~\cite{cajal1909}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Law of Space Conservation (Branching Angle Optimality)}: Optimal branching minimizes intracellular conduction volume:
    \begin{equation}
    \cos \theta^* = \frac{r_0^2 + r_1^2 - r_2^2}{2 r_0 r_1}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Law of Conduction Time Conservation (Branch-Order Delay)}: Centrifugal branch-order delay penalties:
    \begin{equation}
    P_{\text{time}}(k, d) = \exp(-\alpha_{\text{order}} k) \cdot \exp(-d / \lambda_{\text{soma}})
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Law of Material Conservation (Murray's Tapering)}: Continuous caliber scaling $|r_p - r_c| / \max(r_p, r_c)$.
\end{enumerate}
